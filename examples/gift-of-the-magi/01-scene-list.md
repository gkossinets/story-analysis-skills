# Step 3 checkpoint: scene list and dramatis personae

*This is what `crews-analysis` shows before it does any scene-level work. The analysis proceeds only after the user confirms or adjusts the breakdown. It is kept here to show the checkpoint; the final analysis is in `gift-of-the-magi-crews-analysis.md`.*

**Scene list** (7 scenes; the low end for a short story, which fits a 2,000-word piece with one main location)

```
01. The Youngs' flat, afternoon of Christmas Eve — Della — counts $1.87, cries, lets down her hair before the pier-glass and resolves to sell it
02. Mme. Sofronie's, one flight up, same afternoon — Della, Madame Sofronie — sells her hair for $20
03. The stores, the next two hours — Della — finds and buys a platinum fob chain for Jim's watch
04. The flat, early evening to 7 o'clock — Della — curls her cropped hair, readies dinner, waits by the door and prays
05. The flat, 7 p.m. — Della, Jim — Jim stares at her cropped hair; she pleads; he embraces her
06. The flat, continuous — Della, Jim — the gift exchange: combs for hair that is gone, a chain for a watch that is sold
07. Coda, no setting — the narrator — explains the magi and declares the two the wisest givers
```

**Dramatis personae**

- Della — protagonist; Mrs. James Dillingham Young; "Dell" to Jim
- Jim — Della's husband; Mr. James Dillingham Young; "the lord of the flat"; "boy" (Della, 05)
- Madame Sofronie — hair-goods dealer; "Madame"
- The narrator — intrusive first-person commentator; addresses the reader as "you" and "dear friends"

**Boundary calls worth checking**

- 04 merges the curling and the wait. The time jump to 7 o'clock doesn't change the dramatic state (Della alone, anticipating Jim's judgment), so the two are treated as one scene.
- 05 and 06 happen in one room in continuous time. They are split because the kind of action changes when Jim throws the package on the table: a confrontation over the hair becomes a gift-exchange ritual.
- 07 has no characters and no location. It is kept as its own unit because it performs the story's final reversal (of judgment rather than fortune).
