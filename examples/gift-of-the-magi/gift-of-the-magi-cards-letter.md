**COVER — The Gift of the Magi by O. Henry**

**Premise:** On Christmas Eve a poor young married couple each secretly sell their most prized possession to buy the other a gift, and each sale makes the other's gift useless.

**Characters:**

- Della — protagonist and focal character; wants a present worthy of Jim; gets the fob chain, made useless by his sale of the watch (→ her sacrifice is matched, not wasted)
- Jim — her husband, held offstage until 05; wants to give Della the combs she has longed for; gets them to her, useless without her hair (→ meets the double loss with calm and comic understatement)
- Madame Sofronie — gatekeeper; converts the hair into cash
- The narrator — controls what the reader knows and when; turns the plot's verdict ("foolish") into the moral one ("wisest")

**Implied reader:** an early-1900s American newspaper reader who knows the Nativity story, New York prices and places, and the conventions of the sentimental Christmas tale

**Implied author:** affectionate and ironic toward everything except the couple's love, which is finally exempted from irony

**Tone:** comic-sentimental; mock-heroic narration over real distress, turning homiletic in the coda

**Story shape:** twist-ending arc with no antagonist; maps equally well onto kishotenketsu (01 introduction; 02–04 development; 05–06 twist; 07 reconciliation)

**Key motifs:** exact prices ($1.87, $8, $20, $21, 87 cents); the two treasures; the mirror; royal and biblical treasure (Sheba, Solomon, the magi); counting; the chops

**Formats used:** Christmas gift-giving; the commercial sale and purchase; the evening homecoming and dinner

**Control level:** the wage economy ($20 a week), never argued with; the calendar obligation of Christmas; the narrator, who stage-manages what the reader may know

**Intended effect:** the reader laughs at the futility of the two gifts and is then asked to see the futile sacrifices as the true gift

---

**Scene 01 — The Youngs' flat, afternoon of Christmas Eve**

**Present:** Della

**Action:** Della counts $1.87, cries, and at the pier-glass resolves (without saying so) to sell her hair.

**A:** a present worthy of Jim

**B:** months of saving have produced $1.87 (reveal)

**C:** resolves on the sacrifice, shown only through her body (shining eyes, lost colour, a tear on the carpet)

**Stakes:** moral and emotional; whether she can give something "worthy of the honour of being owned by Jim"

**Subtext:** the decision is never stated

**Knowledge:** the two treasures are introduced together at the moment she lets her hair down, so the reader infers the plan before it is acted on

**Plants ↓:** the two treasures, hair and the gold watch; Sheba and Solomon; the $20 weekly wage

**Function:** setup; inciting

**Mood:** comic narration over real misery; grey cat, grey fence, grey backyard

**Compelling because:** the narrator's jokes keep the pathos from going maudlin, and the unspoken decision makes the reader a co-conspirator

---

**Scene 02 — Mme. Sofronie's, one flight up, same afternoon**

**Present:** Della, Madame Sofronie

**Format/ritual:** a commercial sale, cut down to four lines of dialogue

**Action:** Della sells her hair for twenty dollars.

**A:** money for the present

**B:** $20, with no bargaining (reveal: the plan in action)

**Stakes:** the cut is irreversible; after this the choice can't be undone

**Subtext:** Della's haste ("Give it to me quick") keeps her from feeling the loss

**Knowledge:** one-sided dramatic irony begins; Jim will not know what the reader knows

**Payoffs ↑:** hair as treasure (01)

**Function:** rising; the point of no return

**Mood:** cold, commercial, fast

**Compelling because:** after the digressive opening, the speed of the sale lands as a shock

---

**Scene 03 — The stores, the next two hours**

**Present:** Della

**Action:** Della searches the shops and buys a platinum fob chain for Jim's watch for $21.

**A:** a gift worthy of Jim

**B:** one "even worthy of The Watch" (fulfilment, not reversal)

**Stakes:** whether her sacrifice can be redeemed in an object

**Knowledge:** misdirection; the watch is foregrounded as the reason the gift is perfect, never as something Jim might part with

**Plants ↓:** the chain depends on the watch; the old leather strap and Jim's embarrassment; 87 cents left

**Payoffs ↑:** The Watch (01)

**Function:** rising

**Mood:** euphoric ("rosy wings")

**Compelling because:** the more exactly the chain fits the watch, the harder the later reversal will hit; the scene's satisfaction is the trap

---

**Scene 04 — The flat, early evening to 7 o'clock**

**Present:** Della

**Format/ritual:** household routine (coffee made, chops ready, Jim never late)

**Action:** Della curls her cropped hair, fears Jim's reaction, readies dinner and waits by the door, praying.

**A:** repair "the ravages made by generosity added to love"

**B:** curls that make her look like "a truant schoolboy" (partial)

**C:** her hope shrinks to a prayer

**Stakes:** at their peak; whether Jim will still find her pretty

**Subtext:** "If Jim doesn't kill me" is comic hyperbole carrying real fear

**Knowledge:** suspense by postponement; the reader waits for Jim's reaction to a known fact

**Plants ↓:** "Please God, make him think I am still pretty"; the chops

**Function:** rising; the wait before the expected climax

**Mood:** anxious domestic quiet; time narrowing toward 7

**Compelling because:** it builds suspense toward the wrong climax (Jim's verdict on her hair), which the story will step around

---

**Scene 05 — The flat, 7 p.m.: Jim comes home**

**Present:** Della, Jim

**Format/ritual:** the homecoming (wife by the door, dinner ready), disrupted

**Action:** Jim stares at Della's cropped head with an expression she cannot read; she pleads; he wakes from his trance and embraces her.

**A:** reassurance

**B:** bewilderment ("You say your hair is gone?"), then the embrace (non-reversal: the feared rejection never comes)

**Stakes:** shift from Della's looks to an open question about Jim

**Subtext:** Jim's stare hides something that neither Della nor the reader can read

**Knowledge:** the dramatic irony flips; Jim now knows something Della and the reader don't; the narrator looks away during the embrace (screening)

**Plants ↓:** Jim's unreadable stare; "This dark assertion will be illuminated later on"

**Payoffs ↑:** Della's prayer (04), apparently answered

**Function:** the expected climax, defused early

**Mood:** bewildered and tense, then tender

**Compelling because:** the reader shares Della's inability to read Jim, and the narrator's open promise turns the embrace into a cliffhanger

---

**Scene 06 — The flat, continuous: the gifts**

**Present:** Della, Jim

**Format/ritual:** the gift exchange, completed and voided at once

**Action:** Jim gives Della the tortoise-shell combs she can no longer wear; she gives him the chain; he reveals he sold the watch to buy the combs.

**A:** Della wants Jim to see his present

**B:** the watch is gone (reversal; the second of two, after the combs)

**C:** the gifts are put away "a while"; "suppose you put the chops on"

**Stakes:** the material loss is total and shared; the emotional stake is settled

**Knowledge:** recognition; Della and the reader catch up together, and Jim's stare (05) is explained after the fact

**Payoffs ↑:** the watch (01, 03); the chain (03); Jim's stare (05); the chops (04); Della's prayer, now answered in words (04)

**Function:** twist; climax

**Mood:** ecstatic scream, hysterical tears, then quiet comedy

**Compelling because:** the second reversal mirrors the first so exactly that it lands as a joke and a revelation at once; Jim's turn to dinner keeps it from melodrama

---

**Scene 07 — Coda: the narrator on the magi**

**Present:** none (the narrator speaks)

**Action:** The narrator explains who the magi were and declares the "two foolish children" the wisest of all givers.

**A:** the narrator calls the chronicle "uneventful" and the couple foolish

**B:** he reverses his own verdict: "They are the magi" (reversal of judgment rather than fortune)

**Knowledge:** the moral is declared, not dramatised; repetition does the persuading

**Payoffs ↑:** "This dark assertion will be illuminated later on" (05); Sheba and Solomon's treasures (01); the title

**Function:** reconciliation; moral

**Mood:** from wry to benedictory

**Compelling because:** the narrator stages a reversal of judgment that mirrors the plot's reversal of fortune

**RHYTHM MAP — The Gift of the Magi**

**Reveal/reversal rhythm** (r = reveal, R = reversal, N = non-reversal):

r r r r N R R

**Notes:** four low-key reveals, then all the reversals at once. The non-reversal at 05 defuses the climax the reader expected and clears the ground for the one they didn't (06). The two closing reversals work on different axes (fortune, then judgment), which keeps the cluster from tipping into melodrama.

**Suspense:** postponement (04–05: waiting for Jim's reaction); screening (05: Jim's stare and the narrator's averted gaze); misdirection (01–04: the narration stays with Della, so her secret looks like the only one)

**Dangling cause map:**

- ↓ Della's hair (01) → ↑ sold (02); combs useless (06)
- ↓ Jim's gold watch (01) → ↑ chain bought for it (03); sold for the combs (06)
- ↓ the leather strap (03) → ↑ the chain is useless (06)
- ↓ "make him think I am still pretty" (04) → ↑ the embrace (05); "like my girl any less" (06)
- ↓ Jim's unreadable stare (05) → ↑ explained by the combs (06)
- ↓ "illuminated later on" (05) → ↑ the magi coda (07)
- ↓ Sheba's jewels, Solomon's treasures (01) → ↑ the magi's gifts (07)
- ↓ the chops (04) → ↑ "suppose you put the chops on" (06)

**Unresolved:**

- Jim's need for an overcoat and gloves (05)
- The 87 cents (03)
- The combs are put away "a while"; the hair will grow back, but the watch is gone for good

**Status and power arcs:**

- Della: holds information power (her secret) through 01–04 → dependent on Jim's verdict at 04–05 → an equal giver at 06
- Jim: a name on a card with the earner's positional power (01) → holds the verdict at 05 → gives it up with his confession at 06
- The narrator: controls knowledge throughout; at 07 converts that control into moral authority

**Mood arc:**

- grey misery (01) → cold commerce (02) → euphoria (03) → dread (04) → bewilderment, then tenderness (05) → hysteria, then calm comedy (06) → benediction (07)
